hi, all. happy new years! it's been a couple weeks since my previous post here, but i'm back. before i say anything, i need to make it clear that i am a **supporter** of open-source software, and that this article is in no way an attack on the open-source ecosystem. with that being said, today i want to touch on something that irks me a lot; **the way some people behave within open-source**.

> what does that mean, though? you're gonna have to be a bit more specific.

okay, i have a bit of a scenario for you:

let's say you are using something like the [i3 window manager](https://i3wm.org/) and you want to achieve something in specific, but cannot find it in the documentation. let's assume that thing is the ability to maximize windows instead of fullscreening them (e.g. so you can allow the window to cover the whole screen while still seeing other widgets like your panel).

naturally, you would probably just google it, thinking to yourself "okay, i'm probably just super blind and somebody in a forum will be able to redirect me to the proper solution." instead, what you are met with leaves you questioning reality.

this was the exact scenario i faced. i found a [proposal](https://github.com/i3/i3/issues/2107) on [GitHub](https://github.com/) that attempted to address this exact problem. however, there was one tiny problem with it:

![](https://i.postimg.cc/D0NB5QtR/image.png)

^ this very thing is what makes me so unbelievably mad. i see it everywhere, and i believe that it ultimately **goes against the philosophy of open-source**. open-source is meant to allow anybody to add or request any feature within a codebase—large or small—and form constructive discussions around it. and, i hate to break it to you, but "i'm not sure why you would want to have `[...]`" is **not constructive at all**, *especially* if you are not the maintainer because you're at the same level as the proponent and cannot speak on behalf of other users. it makes you sound egotistical and narcissistic; as if the way you utilize the tool is better than the way the proponent (or everybody else seeking the same change) does.

if you need to explain why something is not practical and that there is another way to solve the problem the proponent is facing, you **acknowledge and validate the demand for the request** and then direct them to the proper way to get around their problem instead of saying something that comes across as "ah, yeah, i don't really see a need for this and my opinion is the ultimatum and i said so. therefore, you should feel ashamed for making this post."

if you thought the post in the screenshot wasn't bad enough, here's this:

![](https://i.postimg.cc/9M0W7CVg/image.png)

i barely even have words for this. "ah, yes; to get around this issue, you should do the exact thing that the proponent (and many others) are trying to avoid doing because, once again, my opinion is the ultimatum and i said so, therefore you should feel ashamed for making this post." and the icing on the cake is the random new thread member saying "yeah, i agree with Mr. Ultimatum", refusing to elaborate, and then closing the post as "completed" himself despite tens of people still actively trying to discuss it. the whole thing just blows my mind.

> yeah, but why is this an actual issue? it's annoying, but does it actually affect anything massively?

this whole thing highlights a much bigger problem with open-source communities; a lot of the people who interact within these spaces are **programmers**, not UX designers, not social butterflies, not negotiators, just programmers. they don't understand that when creating a product they are not the only consumer, and therefore their wants/needs are not the only ones that need to be fulfilled. yet, they choose to add their two cents where it clearly doesn't belong because their narcissism tells them to do so.

more importantly, this discourages other users with actual issues and valid proposals from actually speaking up about them. they see how other likeminded people are treated within these forums, and they become scared of looking stupid. this is the exact thing that planted such a deep social stigma into services like [Stack Overflow](https://stackoverflow.com), and soon the most traditionally-constructive places like GitHub will follow suit if people don't get their acts together.
